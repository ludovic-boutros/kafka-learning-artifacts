The isolated leader: from link cut to truncation

Step through one partition while the DC-A ↔ DC-B link is down and then restored. Watch who leads, which ISR each side believes in, where the high watermark sits, and which records survive. Choose a scenario: the active controller in DC-A or DC-B at the moment of the cut, or a split brain caused by forcing a quorum in DC-B while DC-A is still alive.

Assumptions: 4 brokers split 2 + 2, 3 KRaft controllers split 2 (DC-A) + 1 (DC-B), with the active controller in DC-A or DC-B. Broker 3 in DC-B leads the partition at epoch 7. Kafka defaults (broker.session.timeout.ms 9 s, replica.lag.time.max.ms 30 s, controller.quorum.fetch.timeout.ms 2 s, controller.quorum.election.timeout.ms 1 s), min.insync.replicas ≤ 2, unclean leader election disabled. In the split-brain scenario, an operator force-recovers controller 3 as a single-voter quorum while DC-A is still running. Offsets are illustrative.

What to remember

Two leaders, only one can commit

Only the quorum side commitsThe isolated leader can't shrink its ISR without the controller, so its high watermark stays frozen. Nothing new is committed there.
acks=1 in the cut-off DC loses dataThose writes are acknowledged above the frozen high watermark, then truncated after the heal. Nothing reports an error.
acks=all + idempotence is safeProducers only see timeouts and retries. Offset commits surface as COORDINATOR_NOT_AVAILABLE until clients reach the new leader.
The active controller's location only shifts timingIf it sits in the minority DC, it resigns after about 2 s and DC-A elects a new one. Failover takes a few seconds longer, but the outcome is the same.
Split brain needs a manual overrideKafka never builds a second quorum by itself. It happens when someone forces one in DC-B while DC-A is still alive. Both sides then commit, and one side's acknowledged writes are lost at recovery. Confirm the other site is really down and keep it down first, or use a 2.5 DC tie-breaker.
min ISR decides the quorum side tooWith RF = 4 split 2 + 2, the new ISR is {1, 2}. If min.insync.replicas is 3, DC-A also rejects acks=all writes and commits.

Sources

Behavior is taken from Apache Kafka documentation, KIPs and source code. The topology, timings and offsets are illustrative assumptions.

  • KIP-497 and apache/kafka PR #9100: only the controller changes the ISR. The leader keeps the previous ISR until a shrink is confirmed (the "maximal" ISR).
  • KIP-101 and KIP-279: leader epochs, OffsetForLeaderEpoch and follower truncation.
  • GroupCoordinatorService error handling: NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS / REQUEST_TIMED_OUT are returned to clients as COORDINATOR_NOT_AVAILABLE.
  • Apache Kafka broker configs: min.insync.replicas, unclean.leader.election.enable, controller quorum settings.
  • KIP-595: Raft for the metadata quorum. A metadata record is committed once a majority of voters has it.
  • Apache Kafka configuration: controller.quorum.fetch.timeout.ms (a leader resigns without fetches from a majority) and controller.quorum.election.timeout.ms.
  • Confluent for Kubernetes: recover a multi-region KRaft cluster from quorum loss: force-standalone recovery creates a new metadata timeline. If the failed region's controllers come back and can reach each other, they form a second, competing quorum (split brain).
  • Confluent multi-datacenter architectures: a 2.5 DC layout (a light third site with controllers only) keeps the quorum through any single DC failure.
  • Aiven-Open/kraft-last-standing: an open-source tool that shrinks a lost quorum to one surviving voter. Its docs warn that such recovery locks in data loss.
  • KRaft operations: controller quorum and the 3-controller recommendation.
